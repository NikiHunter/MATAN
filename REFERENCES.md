# Recommended references

The course notes are original, concise explanations. The following books are recommended for additional proofs, alternative viewpoints, and extensive problem practice. No chapter is intended to reproduce a textbook.

## Introductory and rigorous calculus

1. Terence Tao, *Analysis I* and *Analysis II* — construction of the real numbers, rigorous calculus, metric spaces, several-variable analysis, and Lebesgue integration.
2. Walter Rudin, *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* — a compact treatment of real and complex analysis foundations.
3. Vladimir A. Zorich, *Mathematical Analysis I–II* — a broad course with geometric and physical motivation.
4. Г. М. Фихтенгольц, *Курс дифференциального и интегрального исчисления*, тт. 1–3 — detailed classical theory and examples.
5. Tom M. Apostol, *Mathematical Analysis* — calculus, metric spaces, and selected advanced topics.

## Problems

6. Б. П. Демидович, *Сборник задач и упражнений по математическому анализу* — a large graded problem collection.
7. G. Polya and G. Szegő, *Problems and Theorems in Analysis* — deeper problems and methods.

## Measure, integration, and functional analysis

8. H. L. Royden and P. M. Fitzpatrick, *Real Analysis* — measure, integration, and function spaces.
9. A. N. Kolmogorov and S. V. Fomin, *Introductory Real Analysis* — metric spaces, measure, and functional analysis.
10. Elias M. Stein and Rami Shakarchi, *Real Analysis* — measure theory, integration, and Hilbert spaces.

## Fourier analysis and vector calculus

11. Elias M. Stein and Rami Shakarchi, *Fourier Analysis* — Fourier series and their applications.
12. Michael Spivak, *Calculus on Manifolds* — rigorous multivariable integration and differential forms.

Use the edition available to you; theorem numbering and terminology vary between editions and translations.
